# 📘 Study Plan — AWS Certified AI Practitioner (AIF-C01)

Five weeks, ending with the exam on **3 October 2026**.

| Week | Dates | Focus | What that means |
|:----:|-------|-------|-----------------|
| **1** | 31 Aug – 6 Sep | Orientation | Read the official exam guide and its domain weightings first, so the rest is aimed at something. Confirm what the prep plan currently contains. |
| **2** | 7 – 13 Sep | Core concepts | AI, ML, deep learning and generative AI as the exam distinguishes them. The AWS services that carry those ideas. **Book the exam on 12 September.** |
| **3** | 14 – 20 Sep | Depth and hands-on | Foundation models, prompting, retrieval-augmented generation, fine-tuning. Responsible AI: bias, fairness, explainability, governance. Labs where a lab exists — running it beats watching it. |
| **4** | 21 – 27 Sep | Practice questions | The official question set, worked slowly. Every wrong answer gets written down and the topic behind it goes back on the list. The score is not the point; the gaps are. |
| **5** | 28 Sep – 3 Oct | Simulation | The official pretest in one sitting, ninety minutes, no notes. Then four days on whatever it exposes. |

---

**The exam guide comes first, not the courses.** It says which domains carry the most weight, and without that you study evenly across topics that are not weighted evenly.

**The booking date matters more than the exam date.** 12 September sits in the middle of the plan on purpose. An exam with no date on it drifts, and this window closes when the semester opens.

**If the pretest is close, move the exam.** A narrow pass five days out is a warning, not a reassurance. A failed attempt costs more than four weeks of waiting.

The exact modules are not listed here. AWS is restructuring its exam prep plans, and a list copied down today would be wrong by the time it mattered — so the plan names what to cover, and the current course list is read from the platform each time.
